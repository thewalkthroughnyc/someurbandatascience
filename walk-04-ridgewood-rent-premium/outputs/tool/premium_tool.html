What this can't tell you

It's a description, not a cause. Being near the M doesn't make rent go up. Station locations and neighborhood change are tangled together, and one snapshot can't pull them apart.

"Near the M" and "in Ridgewood" are hard to separate. Every M-only station sits in one corner of the map, so the gradient may be picking up the neighborhood's own trajectory as much as the train.

Redrawing the sample around the M weakens it. Built on a tighter band, the effect points the same way but isn't statistically significant — the wide sample's power comes from including tracts far from any M stop.

These are tract medians, not listings. Census five-year figures include rent-stabilized units, so what a mover faces today is probably steeper than what's drawn here.

Method. ln(rent) regressed on network walk minutes to the nearest M-only station, holding distance to other nearby stations, building age, median rooms, renter share and unit mix fixed. HC1 robust standard errors.
